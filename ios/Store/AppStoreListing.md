## App privacy

### Privacy Policy URL

```
https://uwyn.com/privacy.html
```

The policy has a paragraph about the app: "Gates of Humanity, our app for Human Design charts,
does not collect anything. The charts are calculated inside the app. The people you save stay
in the app and in your own iCloud, which keeps them the same on your devices and which we
can't look into, and the app makes no connection to us or to anyone else."

### Data collection

```
No, we do not collect data from this app
```

The app makes no network connection of its own: the charts are calculated on the device, and the saved people are kept inside the app, which shares them with its own widgets on the same device and with the person's own iCloud, through the key-value store of iCloud, so that they're the same on their devices. Nobody but that person can read them there, so nothing is collected. A file of exported people, a saved image and a shared link only go where the person sends them, through the sheet of iOS. Saving an image adds to the photos, which iOS asks permission for, and the app can't read them.

## Review risks

- A reviewer can read an app that draws its screens in a web view as a wrapped website (guideline 4.2). The review
  notes say what's true: nothing is loaded from the web and it all works in airplane mode.
- Apple counts fortune telling apps among the crowded kinds that need to stand out (guideline 4.3), and a reviewer
  may see a chart app that way. The description leads with what sets this one apart: it's all calculated on the
  device, it's free without a subscription or an account, and every part of a chart explains itself.
- Jovian Archive claims "Rave Mandala" and "Rave BodyGraph". The listing avoids both, and so does the app, except
  for one description that says what the outer rings of the wheel are called. The tagline of the title bar, which
  an iPad shows, says "Wheel of the 64 Gates".
